## Source screening — applied before anything below was kept

`recruit_oral_board_sources.md` established the rule: being on a city website is not
sufficient screening; check who authored it. This pass extends the rule to a source class
the first pass did not cover — **free online prep material** — and the answer is that it
is the worst source class available to us, for two independent reasons.

**Reason one: it is somebody's compilation.** An individual short interview question
("Why do you want to be a firefighter?") is a fact about hiring practice and is not
anybody's property. A curated, ordered list of fifty of them is an authored work. The
FireRescue1 list carries "Copyright © 2026 Lexipol." The Station Visit question page is a
**direct competitor's** compilation. Copying either is copying the part that is protected
while skipping the part that is free.

**Reason two: prep vendors are the least reliable narrators about oral boards.**
`recruit_design_decisions.md` §8 already says this about anchors — vendors have a
commercial interest in presenting the board as decodable. It applies at least as hard to
question lists, where the incentive is to look comprehensive rather than to be accurate.

**How prep material is used here:** as corroboration of *themes only*, never as a source
of items, and never as a source of ordering or taxonomy. A family below earns its place
from the department-authored and apprenticeship-authored sources; the trade-press list is
recorded as a tally mark confirming the theme recurs, and the competitor's taxonomy is
recorded under "market check" and was deliberately read **after** the family list was
drafted from clean sources.

## Sizing

### The requirement

`recruit_design_decisions.md` §4: novel question every session, no preview, no re-record,
no repeats. So the bank must outlast a subscription.

### The arithmetic

| Usage assumption | Sessions/yr | 1 question/session | 5-question full board |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| Light — weekly | 52 | 52 | 260 |
| Target — 3×/week | 156 | 156 | 780 |
| Heavy — daily | 365 | 365 | 1,825 |

The full-board column is the honest one if we ship a full mock panel (which we should —
see `badgeday_pricing.md`, it is table stakes against Station Visit). Naively that means
**~780 reviewed items for a year of target-intensity use**, which reads like exactly the
ever-growing library `CLAUDE.md` rejects.

### Why it isn't

**A Recruit question is an unkeyed prompt.** It has no answer, no distractors, no
citation, and no correctness to verify. Compare a Promote item, which needs a stem, four
options, a defensible key, and a citation resolving to a stored chunk. The review question
for a Recruit prompt is only: *is this a fair, realistic, generalizable board question,
and which criteria does it load?* That is seconds of a captain's attention, not minutes.

**And it parameterizes.** The judgment lives in the family, not the variant. Family C1
("a specific conflict with a peer or a supervisor") supports variants that differ in
who, when, and what was at stake — a supervisor rather than a peer, a conflict you lost, a
conflict where you were wrong, one that never resolved — and every one of those is the
same board question wearing different clothes. **The SME reviews 36 families; the variants
are a batch skim.**

So:

> **Recommended target: 36 families × 8 variants ≈ 290 prompts at launch, growing to
> ~500 within six months of launch.**
>
> Review cost: 36 family reviews (the real work — call it a weekend) plus a batch pass
> over variants. The reviewable asset stays bounded, exactly as the rubric architecture
> intends.

At 290 prompts a full-board user gets **~58 sessions** — over a year at weekly cadence,
about four months at target cadence. At 500 it is ~100 sessions. That is sufficient for
launch and the growth path is cheap.

**Do not chase a very large bank before the rubrics are publishable.** C4 is Grant-approved
/ publishable 2026-09-10. C1/C5 structure drafts exist (PR 46) but are not publishable
until adjacent bands are rewritten; C3 still owes a text pass. Prompts the product cannot
critique against a reviewed criterion are inventory, not product. The order that maximises
value per unit of your review time is: finish C1/C5 (and the C3 pass) → variants across
the families those criteria unlock → more families.

### Exhaustion

A heavy user will eventually run out. Three options, in order of preference:

1. **12-month retirement.** A prompt a candidate saw a year ago is effectively novel —
   he has not rehearsed it and cannot recall the wording. Cheapest, no new review surface,
   and it makes the effective bank unbounded for any realistic subscription length.
   **Recommended.**
2. **Reserve pool.** Hold ~15% of the bank out of general rotation and release it to
   long-tenured subscribers, so the people most invested are not the ones who hit the
   bottom. Cheap, worth doing alongside (1).
3. **Generate variants at runtime from a reviewed family template.** Held in reserve and
   **not recommended for V1**: it puts model-authored text in front of a candidate, and
   even though a prompt is far lower-risk than a critique, it crosses the review gate and
   should be its own decision rather than a quiet fallback.

### Rotation policy

- **Never repeat a prompt** to the same candidate. Hard rule, enforced in the schema.
- **Never repeat a family within a rolling window of 8 sessions.** Without this, "conflict"
  can surface three sessions running from three different variants and the practice stops
  feeling like a board.
- **Selection is 70% weighted / 30% random.** Weight toward families loading the criteria
  where the candidate's tracked behaviors are weakest; keep a real random component so the
  next question stays genuinely unpredictable. A fully weighted selector becomes guessable,
  which reintroduces rehearsal through the back door.
- **A full-board session draws one from each of A, B/C, D/E/F, G/H, J** — mirroring the
  intro → behavioral → scenario → values → closing shape every source describes. Never five
  from one group.

## Authoring

Bank items are **written in original words**, like everything else here. The families above
are the evidence that a theme is genuinely asked; they are not item text to be copied, and
the two clean primary sources are 17 questions total, which is nowhere near a bank anyway.

Each item carries:

- `family` — one of the 36 above
- `criteria` — which rubric criteria it loads
- `variant_axis` — what this variant changes relative to its siblings
- `source_basis` — the families' source tally, for the review record
- `review` — reviewer, date, verdict, per the standing gate

**Claude may draft items into the review chain** (`recruit_scope.md` step 1 precedent:
drafting is permitted, publishing without SME review is not). Nothing here publishes
unreviewed.
