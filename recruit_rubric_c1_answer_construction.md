## Why this criterion is scored across the whole board

Answer construction is a habit, not a performance on one prompt. A candidate can nail a
single well-rehearsed answer and still wander, over-claim, or never stop across the rest of
the board. Scoring this criterion on one designated question is how the rehearsed answer
escapes, and how the pattern of missing the question asked never shows up.

So there is **no designated Answer Construction question**. Every answer on the board is
evidence. The tell is distributional: does he answer what was asked, does the answer have a
shape, are claims specific, does he stop when done, and will he say what he got wrong —
*across* the board, not once.

**Practice sessions issue a full board of five questions** (settled 2026-09-10). Whole-board
scoring is not provisional. Do not score this criterion from a single answer as if it were
the board.

**Anchors below describe patterns across answers. They must not be applied to a single
answer in isolation, and they must not name or imply a specific prompt.**

## What this criterion can and cannot see

This product scores from **audio / transcript**. That is half of what a live panel scores
under the old name “Communication,” and it is not the whole of it.

**Audio can see (this criterion):**

- Did he answer what was asked, or a neighboring question he preferred.
- Does the answer have a shape — a point, support, a close — or does it drift.
- Are claims specific (people, places, actions, outcomes) or only general.
- Does he stop when the answer is finished, or keep talking past it / cut so short there
  is nothing to check.
- Will he say what he got wrong or left incomplete, without being invited to.
- Pace, fluency, and length (see Delivery sub-score).

**Audio cannot see (not this criterion — work with a person):**

- Presence in the room.
- Eye contact.
- Nerves / composure that does not show up in the voice.
- Likeability / whether a crew would want him at the table.

**Do not invent scores for the second list.** If critique mentions them, it may only say
they are outside what this tool can see and belong with a mentor, a mock board, or a live
panel — never as a finding about this candidate from the audio.

## Scoring notes

These govern how the anchors below are applied. They are part of the criterion.

**1. Answer what was asked outranks polish.**

An answer that is rough but on the question asked outranks a smooth answer to a different
question. The first job is to address the prompt in front of him. Reciting a prepared story
that does not fit is a construction failure, not a delivery success.

**2. Shape is structure, not theatre.**

A shaped answer has a point, something that supports it, and a stop. It does not need a
named framework, a mnemonic, or a performance arc. A plainly ordered answer that lands is
better construction than a polished one that never reaches a point.

**3. Specificity is a credibility floor, not vocabulary.**

Claims that name what happened, who was there, what he did, or what changed can be checked.
Claims that only describe how he generally believes he communicates cannot. Score whether
there is purchase on something real — not whether the story is well told.

**4. Stopping is part of construction.**

An answer that keeps going after it has landed costs him here, not only on the Delivery
sub-score. So does an answer so short that nothing can be checked. Length is judged against
the question asked, not against a fixed clock.

**5. Saying what he got wrong is scored when it appears; absence is not mind-reading.**

Where he notices a miss, a half-answer, or a claim he cannot stand behind, and says so, that
is positive evidence at the top of the band. Where he does not, **do not invent that he
knows he was wrong.** Score what he said. Critique may note that a panel often listens for
whether a candidate will own a miss — tied to something audible in this board — without
claiming an internal state.

**6. Three outcomes, not two.**

Rubric-wide rule, settled on Criterion 3 and restated on Criterion 2. Stated here for this
criterion.

**Where he gave nothing to judge either way, return *not answered*.** Silence, a refusal, a
request to repeat with no follow-through, a pivot that never returns to the question. Not a
low score and not a finding about him — it says only that this material did not reach the
criterion. On a whole-board criterion, *not answered* applies when there is no usable
spoken material across the board to judge construction from.

**Where the material positively establishes that construction cannot be assessed, return
*insufficient evidence to assess*.** Its own outcome, never averaged in as a zero, and it
must quote what establishes it. On this criterion that is rare: a failed transcript, audio
too corrupted to follow, or answers so fragmentary that no claim, shape, or stop can be
read. **Getting this backwards tells a man his answers have no construction, on evidence
nobody has.**

**It is not an escape hatch for anchor 1.** Thin, off-question, or endless answers *have*
construction — it is poor, and poor is what anchor 1 scores. Insufficient evidence requires
that there is nothing scorable, not that what is there is weak.

**7. Critique voice constraint.**

Generated critique may state how an answer is **likely to read to a panel**, tied to a
specific observable behavior in the audio or transcript. It may not assert a score the
candidate received, or attribute an internal state.

- Permitted: "Three answers in a row start on the question and finish on a different one —
  that reads as a prepared story looking for a home."
- Rejected: "You sound nervous." / "You don't come across as confident." / "The panel won't
  like you."

**8. One weak answer does not set the band.**

Because this criterion is whole-board, a single miss, a single ramble, or a single short
answer is evidence, not a sentence. Look for what repeats. A pattern of missing the question
is a 2; one miss inside otherwise on-question answers is not.

**9. Red flags are not contained.**

A construction flag early — never addressing what was asked, or never stopping — is
reported to shape how later answers are read, rather than scored in isolation. Panel
behavior is not answer-independent.

**10. This criterion is partly subjective, and that is not a defect.**

Panels react to how an answer is built. The anchors stay behavioral so they can be
verified; the reaction to those behaviors is named where it bears on the score. Subjectivity
here is not permission to score presence or likeability from audio.

## Anchors

### 5 — Answers what was asked; shaped; specific; stops; owns a miss when it shows

Across the board, he addresses the question in front of him rather than the one he prepared
for. Answers have a clear shape — a point, support, a close — without needing a recited
framework. Claims land on people, places, actions, or outcomes rather than adjectives alone.
He finishes when the answer is finished. Where something in his own answer is wrong,
incomplete, or stronger than he can defend, he says so without being invited.

**Pattern, not a single polished reply.** One clean answer inside a board of drifts is not
a 5. The top band is how he builds answers when the prompts change.

### 4 — Strong construction, short of the top band

On-question, shaped, and specific across most of the board. Claims are checkable more often
than not. He usually stops when the answer is finished. What keeps him out of 5 is
inconsistency on the last steps: shape softens when the prompt changes, or he does not own
a miss when one shows up in his own words, or a minority of answers still wander or stay
general.

**Audible delivery does not move this number.** Pace, fluency, and length that make the
listen hard belong on the Delivery sub-score. A candidate can be a construction 4 with
Clear delivery, or a construction 4 with Costly delivery — same construction band.

**Boundary against 3.** A 4 requires that **most** answers address the question asked and
carry at least one checkable claim. A board of correct-sounding generalities is a 3,
however calmly delivered.

**Boundary against 5.** A 5 keeps shape when the prompt changes and owns a miss when it
appears, as a pattern. A 4 does the core build well and is short on one of those last steps.

### 3 — Competent, general, and finished when the list ends

Answers the question in a way that is hard to call wrong and hard to remember. Shape is
present but thin — often a short list or a single unsupported claim. Specificity is scarce;
the words are right and could belong to anyone. He usually stops near enough to on time. He
does not volunteer what he got wrong.

Distinguish from 4: the 4 has at least some checkable specifics and is mostly on-question.
The 3 is mostly general even when he stays on the prompt. Delivery does not decide this
boundary — it is scored on the Delivery sub-score.

### 2 — Construction fails as a pattern

Across the board, one or more of these repeats:

- Answers a neighboring question he prefers, not the one asked.
- Never reaches a point — starts, digresses, ends without a landing.
- Claims stay abstract with nothing checkable.
- Length is structural: still talking long after the answer ended, or so short there is
  nothing to assess.
- An obvious miss in what he said goes unowned when a panel would expect him to catch it
  (critique may note the miss; do not invent that he noticed).

Distinguish from a 3: the 3 is flat but mostly on-question. The 2 is off-question, shapeless,
or endless/empty as a *habit*, not once.

Distinguish from a 1: the 2 still produces recognizable attempts at answers. The 1 leaves a
panel with nothing usable, repeatedly.

### 1 — Unusable as answers, repeatedly

Does not address what was asked across the board. No shape. Nothing specific. Or so
disordered or endless that a listener cannot follow the point even when one might be buried
in it. This is a pattern of unusable answers, not one collapse inside an otherwise built
board.

## Delivery sub-score (audio-visible only)

Scored separately from the 1–5 construction band so critique can say “the build is fine and
the listen is hard” without collapsing both into one number.

| Band | What it means |
|---|---|
| Clear | Pace and fluency leave the answer easy to follow; length matches the question; stalls, if any, are brief and recovered. |
| Costly | Rushed, heavily stalled, monotone-flat to the point of hard follow, or length that repeatedly buries or truncates the point. Still intelligible. |
| Blocking | So fast, broken, or endless that the construction cannot be followed even when it may be present. If construction cannot be read because of delivery alone, say so — do not silently score construction as 1. |

**This sub-score still cannot see presence, eye contact, nerves-as-demeanor, or likeability.**
Those remain outside the tool.

**Scale settled 2026-09-10:** three bands only (Clear / Costly / Blocking). Do not mirror to
1–5. Do not fold Delivery back into the construction number.

## Cross-references

**Motivation & Preparation → Criterion 2.**
A thin preparation file is not scored here. A beautifully built answer with nothing behind
it may still be a 5 on Answer Construction and a 2 on Motivation — coherent, not a
contradiction.

**Teamwork & Interpersonal → Criterion 3.**
Self-focus in who populates his stories is Criterion 3’s business. This criterion scores how
the answer is built, not who is in it — except insofar as specificity needs *someone* or
*something* checkable (scoring note 3).

**Unscoreable half → a person.**
Presence, eye contact, nerves, likeability: mock board, mentor, station visit, live panel.
The product names the gap; it does not fill it from audio.
