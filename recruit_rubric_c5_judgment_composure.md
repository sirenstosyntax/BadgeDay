## Why this criterion uses a designated question plus corroboration

Panels put deliberate probes here: a high-pressure event he was in, how he expects to handle
the stress of the work, what he has done to stay effective under load, a hard decision and
how he made it, ranking traps where the *reasoning* is the test, and self-awareness asks
(weakness, strength with evidence, what a former employer would name as a negative).

Scoring **only** on that probe misses composure and judgment tells elsewhere — freezing into
a non-answer, padding with “great question,” or a ranking given as if there were a correct
order with no defense.

Scoring **only** whole-board with no designated probe understates that panels ask these on
purpose.

**Grant settled 2026-09-10:** same shape as Criteria 2 and 4 — **designated question
primary; corroboration admissible from the rest of the board.**

**Grant settled 2026-09-10 (captain pass):** corroboration may move the band set by the
designated answer by **at most one** (up or down). It does not jump two bands.

---

## What this criterion is for

A panel is deciding whether this person can **think under pressure and stay usable** — not
whether his voice sounds calm, and not whether he has the ethics core (that is Criterion 4).

**Judgment** here: how he frames a problem, what he weighs, whether he can defend a hard
call, and whether he falls for ranking traps by treating the order as the answer instead of
the reasoning.

**Composure** here: what he *did* or *does* to stay effective when things are hard — in a
real high-pressure event, or in how he has prepared to handle the life. Not vocal tone,
accent, or “sounds nervous.”

**Self-awareness** mostly lands here (Grant): can he name a real weakness with evidence, a
strength that is not free adjectives, what a former employer would criticize, without
collapsing into either self-destruction or self-advertisement. Ownership of an ethics miss
still belongs on Criterion 4 when the stake is truth, theft, impairment, or illegal conduct.

---

## What this criterion must not do

**Do not score how the voice sounds and call it composure.** Accent, pitch, gender-linked
voice quality, and “sounds nervous” are unreliable and biased. Composure is read from
**conduct and content**: what he did under pressure, whether he stayed on the problem, whether
he filled silence with empty stall phrases instead of thinking.

A short beat before answering can be composure. Filling that beat with “that’s a great
question” and then stalling is usually worse — same silence, opposite meaning, distinguished
by what he does with it.

---

## Overlap with Criterion 4 (settled)

Ethics and ownership of costly truth stay on **Criterion 4**. Hard calls under stress,
problem-solving, ranking-trap reasoning, and most self-awareness stay **here**. Do not
double-penalize the same sentence on both without saying which part each score is reading.

## Scoring notes

**1. Reasoning outranks the ranking.**

On priority- or mission-ranking prompts, the panel is scoring how he thinks — what he
weighs, what he would revisit, what he refuses to pretend is settled — not whether he
matched a secret correct order. Treating the ranking as the whole answer is a miss.

**2. Specific past pressure outranks hypothetical calm.**

An occasion he was actually in — what happened, what he did, what he would do differently —
beats a speech about always staying calm. “I handle stress well” cannot be checked.

**3. Composure is effectiveness under load, not emotional absence.**

A candidate who felt fear and still did the next right thing has shown composure. A
candidate who claims he never feels anything has often shown a story problem, not a virtue.

**4. Self-awareness needs purchase.**

A weakness that could belong to anyone (“I care too much”) is thin. A weakness with a
specific cost and what he is doing about it can be scored. A strength needs evidence, not
adjectives. What a former employer would name as a negative is scored on honesty and
specificity — not on whether the negative exists.

**5. Critique voice constraint.**

Generated critique may state how an answer is **likely to read to a panel**, tied to a
specific observable behavior in what he said. It may not assert a score, or attribute an
internal state.

- Permitted: "He gives a mission ranking with no reasons — that reads as hunting for the
  right order instead of showing how he decides."
- Rejected: "You sound anxious." / "You panic under pressure." / "You lack judgment."

**6. Do not invent composure from delivery metrics alone.**

Pace and length belong primarily on Criterion 1’s Delivery sub-score when the issue is
audible build. This criterion may note that he abandoned the problem mid-answer or filled
time without content — that is judgment/composure content, not voice quality.

**7. Three outcomes, not two.**

**Where he gave nothing to judge either way, return *not answered*.**

**Where the material positively establishes that judgment/composure cannot be assessed,
return *insufficient evidence to assess*.** Must quote what establishes it. Example shape:
he has never been in a high-pressure event **and** offers no smaller hard decision or
self-awareness occasion when invited. **Not** a low score for a quiet life.

**It is not an escape hatch for a thin “I stay calm” speech** — that is weak content, scored
on the low bands.

**Default when in doubt is *not answered*.**

**8. Red flags are not contained.**

A judgment collapse — unsafe reasoning he stands behind, or total inability to weigh
tradeoffs — is reported to shape the rest of the board.

**9. This criterion is partly subjective, and that is not a defect.**

Panels react to whether they would trust his head when it counted. Anchors stay behavioral;
reaction is named where it bears on the score. Subjectivity is not permission to score voice
or invent panic.

**10. Corroboration may move the designated band by at most one.**

The designated judgment/composure/self-awareness question is primary. Corroborating tells
from the rest of the board may raise or lower that band by **at most one**. They cannot jump
two bands. The designated answer can carry the band alone when it is clear.

## Anchors

### 5 — Thinks in the open; usable under pressure; self-aware with evidence

On the designated probe he shows how he decides or how he stayed effective: a real
high-pressure occasion with what he did; a hard decision with what he weighed; a ranking
defended by reasons rather than by hunting a correct order; or self-awareness with a
checkable weakness/strength and what followed. He stays on the problem. Corroboration across
the board does not show him abandoning judgment when the prompt gets hard.

### 4 — Right direction, thin in places

Shows real judgment or composure instinct — he weighs something, he describes staying
effective, he names a weakness that is not free — but the occasion is light, the reasons on
a ranking are thin, or self-awareness needs prompting. Still mostly specific. Corroboration
does not contradict him.

**Boundary against 3.** A 4 needs at least one checkable occasion or a clear line of
reasoning — not only “I stay calm” / “I use good judgment.”

**Boundary against 5.** A 5 shows costly, specific thinking or composure under load, and
self-awareness that could be checked. A 4 has the direction and is short on cost, clarity,
or consistency.

### 3 — Correct generalities; little to check

Says he handles stress, makes good decisions, knows his weaknesses — without an occasion, or
with an occasion so thin it could be anyone’s. On ranking traps, gives an order with little
or no defense. A panel hears confidence, not a method.

### 2 — Judgment collapses, or composure is only a claim

Pattern on the designated answer and/or corroboration:

- Ranking or hard call answered as if there is a single correct list, with no reasoning when
  pressed.
- High-pressure story where he is only a bystander to his own calm, or he abandons the
  problem.
- Self-awareness that is all advertisement or all self-abasement with nothing checkable.
- Fills time with stall phrases and never reaches a decision or a next action.

### 1 — Unsafe or unusable reasoning he stands behind, repeatedly

Stands behind a decision process that would clearly put people at risk, refuses to weigh
tradeoffs at all, or treats the composure/judgment probe with contempt. Or shows across the
board that he cannot stay with a hard question long enough to think. This is not “sounded
nervous” — it is content a panel can point at.
