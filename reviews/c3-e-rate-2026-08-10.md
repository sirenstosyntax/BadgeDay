**Asked:** does E have a value or a rate, and did today's edits move it?

## Correcting what I concluded last turn

After the third set run I said E's fall *"is not my edit"*, on the strength of §7's 2026-07-29
figure of **5×3, 4B×2**.

**That was the same error I had just finished withdrawing, one turn later.** A 3-of-5 measurement
is not a rate. Its confidence interval runs from roughly 15% to 95%, so it cannot establish a
baseline, and I used it to exonerate an edit — having recorded in §7 that afternoon that a set
run reports values and a boundary answer has a rate.

**What the two numbers actually support:** 60% at n=5 (July) and 40% at n=20 (now) have
overlapping intervals. **The honest position is that no shift has been demonstrated in either
direction, and July was too small to detect one against.** E was unstable at this boundary before
today's edits and is unstable at it now. Whether today's work moved the rate is unmeasured and
would need an n=20 on the pre-edit rubric to answer — which is possible from git history and
probably not worth the 90 cents, because the ambiguity below is actionable regardless.

## The finding: the determinations disagree about exactly one sentence

This is the outcome §7 calls the good one — *"the variance is not noise, it is a specific rubric
ambiguity, and it is legible."* All three distinct determinations agree on the facts of the
answer and split on one question: **does *"if someone was slow you'd just help them out and
they'd do the same for you next time — that's how it works"* count as a second behaviour?**

| Outcome | On the reciprocity line |
|---|---|
| **5** (×8) | *"describing reciprocal help as an ordinary crew practice rather than a notable achievement"* — **counts it**, and names it as the second of two behaviours in two places |
| **4B** (×11) | *"nothing else in the account shows a second instance of crew conduct"* — **does not count it** |
| **4** (×1) | *"the reciprocity line that follows it is a general rule about the crew rather than a tied occasion, so it does not supply a second one"* — **rejects it, and says why** |

The single 4 is the most useful run in the set, because it states the reasoning the eleven 4Bs
leave implicit.

## And the clause already answered it

The breadth clause listed three ways to supply the second behaviour, the second being *"a
disposition he describes as ordinary rather than notable."* **E's line is that route.** It is also
near-verbatim the example in anchor 5's own opening paragraph — *"you help them out and they do
the same for you next time"* — offered there as top-band conduct.

**So the clause was being read against itself.** The sentence immediately above the list says
*"one exchange does not supply two behaviours"*, and that was generalised into *only incidents
count*, which reads routes 2 and 3 straight out of a clause that had just offered them. Both
readings were genuinely available in the text.

**This is the same failure mode as the being-changed route, for the third time in a day:** a
sentence written to rule out one thing quietly ruling out others that sat near it.

## Fix

The three routes are now numbered, each with an example, followed by two sentences that were
missing:

- **Routes 2 and 3 are not tied to a named occasion, and that is not a defect in them.** A
  disposition counts *because* it is general — a man for whom reciprocity is unremarkable is
  describing a pattern, which is the thing a single incident cannot supply.
- **"One exchange does not supply two behaviours" rules out double-counting the halves of one
  event.** It does not rule out routes 2 and 3, which are how most answers that deserve a 5 will
  clear the clause.

A test pins both, including the misreading by name so it stays named.

**What this fix does not claim.** It removes an ambiguity that the determinations demonstrably
split on. It does not establish that E becomes a stable 5 — that needs an n=20 on the amended
text, and the four earlier edits today all read cleanly before a run contradicted them.

## Not the note 5 question, which is worth knowing

§10 has filed E's wobble under its probe since July: *does "I've genuinely never had a problem
with anyone" pull an answer down?* **None of the three determinations decided on that line.** The
5 says it *"does not undo the specific, believable material already given"*; the 4 says it *"is a
note 5 concern but does not by itself pull this to a 2"*; the 4B mentions it only as leaving
appetite unproven, which is a 4A/4B routing point rather than a band one.

So E's instability today is **not** the unfalsifiable-claim question. That question remains open
and unmeasured, and the answer written to probe it has been answering a different question.

## Disposition

- **E has a rate, not a value**, and a single-run divergence on it is unreadable. That is now
  stated in `recruit_review_compare.py`'s own output.
- **The clause ambiguity is fixed** — the fourth anchor edit today, and the third that repairs an
  over-broad sentence of my own rather than implementing a new ruling.
- **Per §7 this needs the whole set re-run**, and then E at n=20 again to see whether the rate
  moved. Not before: the standing recommendation is still to stop editing anchors and take the
  two SME questions — where note 5's flag lands, and whether 4A/4B survives, which had five
  observations behind it from the third set run alone.
