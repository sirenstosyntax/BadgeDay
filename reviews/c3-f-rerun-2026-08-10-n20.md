## Requirement 5 — the class is reachable, and every firing is out of bounds

**4 of 20 produced a risk** (runs 3, 4, 12, 16), up from 0 of 5. The class works, renders under
its own heading, and passes the gate. That much is real.

**All four quote wording that is present in the answer, which note 10 explicitly excludes.**
The note says a risk *"names something absent from this answer"* and that *"where the wording is
actually in the answer, it is not a risk — it is a fault or a strength, and the anchors govern
it."* Zero of four firings are the absent-wording case the note was written from. Two distinct
misapplications, and they need different rulings:

### (a) Runs 4 and 16 are critiquing the fixture, not the candidate

Both flag the opening *"Same sort of thing happened with a lad called Ryan at the depot"* —
correctly observing that it leans on a story a cold panel has not heard.

**That phrase is a seam in the review set, not candidate behaviour.** F was written as one of
eleven answers and its probe records *"Pairs with C"*; the opening is the fixture author's
connective tissue between two answers in a set. A real candidate answers one question. So the
observation is faithful to the text and worthless as calibration.

**Not fixed, deliberately.** Editing F would change an answer Grant scored blind and break
comparability with every prior run on this set. It is a standing caveat for any answer in the
set that opens with a back-reference, and it means two of the four risk firings measure the
harness.

### (b) Runs 3 and 12 contradict anchor 5, and that one is a judgment call

Both flag the closing *"He's the one who sorted it — I just asked the question"* as risking a
panel hearing modesty and reading his contribution as smaller than it was.

Anchor 5, as amended on 2026-08-10, says that exact line is *"crediting precisely, which is the
conduct at the top of this anchor rather than an admission that he did nothing."* The risk says
it may cost him. **Both can be true** — the anchor scores conduct, the risk is about how a panel
hears it on a retelling — but the rubric currently asserts one and the pipeline is producing the
other, on the same sentence, in the same critique.

**This needs Grant, and it is the interesting question the run produced.** Either the model
should be held to note 10's absent-wording boundary, or note 10 is drawn too narrowly and should
cover *present wording that lands differently in a different room*. The second reading is the
more useful product — but it is a change to what a reuse risk **is**, not a tuning fix, and
widening it puts the risk class into direct tension with the anchors on any line that is both a
strength and a liability.

### The coaching problem underneath is improved, not solved

Counting anything forward-looking — a risk or a gap — **5 of 20 runs gave the candidate
something to act on.** Fifteen are pure credit. Better than 1 of 5, still a critique that tells
a man he is finished with a story he is about to retell.

## Requirement 4 — still untested, and the anti-contradiction rule held

**One inventory gap in twenty runs** (run 5), rendered correctly under *beyond this answer*. It
is a good one: it asks whether he would stay in the conversation when what is being avoided is a
safety shortcut rather than a personal situation, which is a real observation about his evidence
inventory and correctly framed as broader than this answer.

**Run 4's self-contradiction from the n=5 run did not recur.** No run told the candidate he had
not done the thing its determination rests on. The rule held — on one gap, so this is absence of
evidence rather than evidence of absence.

## Requirement 6 — clean, with a near-miss the gate cannot see

No criterion names, anchor names, scoring-note numbers, clause ids or route tags in any
`observation` or `ask` across twenty runs.

**What the gate cannot catch is rubric *content* bleeding into candidate prose.** Run 17 ends a
point with *"what separated Ryan's real problem from a rig short on excuses"* — a mangled
reach for note 1's worked example about a rig going out short, and close to incoherent to a
reader who has never seen the rubric. Run 16's *"not a door opened and left there"* is anchor 3's
phrasing; that one reads fine. The jargon check tests vocabulary, not provenance, and the
provenance leak is the harder failure. Flagged, not fixed — a gate for it would need to diff
prose against the rubric text, and the false-positive cost is high.

## Harness finding: one gate rejection, and the message was wrong

Run 6, `supplies_language`: the model wrote *'the same sort of thing'* — the candidate's own
opening with an article in front of it. Five words, one over `MAX_FOREIGN_QUOTE_WORDS`, so it
was rejected as a scripted phrase. The rejection was **correct** (it is not his exact wording)
and the **explanation was wrong**: the model had misquoted him, not scripted for him, and the
retry text told it to stop handing him language.

**Fixed.** The message now names both possibilities and asks for an exact quote or no quotation
marks. One rejection in twenty runs (5%), and the retry recovered — run 6 still produced three
verified points.

## Disposition

**Requirements 1, 2, 3 and 6 are closed.** Requirement 5's class is built, reachable, and
firing outside its own definition — the boundary question is Grant's. Requirement 4 needs a
fixture that actually produces gaps before it can be called anything.

**Calibration item: closeable on the score, open on the coaching.** Recommend closing the F
divergence and reopening the reuse-risk boundary as its own question rather than carrying it
under F.
