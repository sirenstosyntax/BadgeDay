**Run 4 is worse than a miss — it contradicts itself.** Its determination rests on the
disclosure being the evidence the conversation worked. Its inventory gap then tells the
candidate *"there was no pushing past a first deflection."* Both sentences are in the same
critique. The gap is a defensible observation about his evidence *generally* — he has not
shown himself raising something unwelcome — but it is written as a fact about **this** answer,
which is the exact defect requirement 4 exists to prevent, arriving through a different door.

## Requirement 5 — the risk class never fired

Zero `risk` points across five runs. No *IF YOU USE THIS STORY AGAIN* section rendered. The
clause was citable (`c3.note.10` parses, and a test pins that), the prompt described the class,
and the gate would have passed one. The model simply never reached for it.

**The prompt made it optional and the model took the option.** *"At most one, and only where
there is a real one"* reads as permission, not direction, and nothing told the model that a
strong answer is precisely where the class earns its keep. F is the case note 10 was written
from — the opening self-assessment is absent from the transcript, so it is a reuse risk rather
than a fault — and the model had every piece it needed to say so.

**The larger shape of the failure: four of five runs gave the candidate nothing to act on.**
All credit, no ask, no caution. For an answer that scores 5 that is defensible on the score
and wrong as coaching — he is about to tell this story to another panel. The SME critique for
F gave one practical caution; the pipeline gave none, four times out of five.

## Requirement 6 — passes, with a caveat about the renderer

No rubric vocabulary in any `observation` or `ask`. No *cast-of-story test*, no *this criterion
runs*, no clause ids or route tags in prose. Zero gate rejections across five runs, so the
jargon check did not fire even as a false positive — the guard is narrow enough to live with.

**The caveat is the renderer, not the model.** `render_critique` prints each point's clause id
and the rubric's own heading as the bullet label — *"• [c3.note.2] Score the cast of the story,
not its adjectives."* That is correct for an SME inspection tool and it is where the labels
belong. It also means **the rendered output is not a preview of what a candidate sees.** Any
candidate-facing surface must render `observation` / `ask` / `answer_quote` only. Worth stating
plainly before step 4 builds one, because the inspection view reads like a finished artifact.

## Changes made in response, not yet re-run

Three prompt edits, all aimed at requirements 2 and 5. None touch the anchors — the scoring
behaviour under test is unchanged.

1. **Risk on a strong answer.** The class now says it is usually the only thing left to give
   him and to look for it hardest there, with the reason: a critique that is nothing but credit
   reads as *you are finished with this one*, and he is not.
2. **Name the move, not the category.** *"You asked"* is a category, and candidates who score
   badly also asked. Where the result came from not accepting the first answer, the point has
   to say so in those terms — he cannot repeat a move described only in outline.
3. **No point may contradict the determination.** Aimed at run 4. Where a behaviour is his
   strength here and thin across his evidence generally, the point says it is thin *elsewhere*,
   never that it is absent here.

**Unverified.** These are three sentences in a prompt against one five-run observation, and the
requirement-2 result (2 of 5) is inside the range where five runs cannot distinguish a fix from
noise. The rerun needs more than five.
